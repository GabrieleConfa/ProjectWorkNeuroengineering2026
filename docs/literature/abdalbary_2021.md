# Abdalbary et al. 2021 — Histology of the median nerve in arm, cubital fossa and wrist

**Citation.** Abdalbary SA et al. *The myth of median nerve in forearm and its role in double
crush syndrome: a cadaveric study.* Front. Surg. 8:648779, 2021. doi:10.3389/fsurg.2021.648779.
Open access: PMC8490666. Not in `Papers/`.

**Role in the project.** Only direct measurement of **perineurium thickness in the human median
nerve** that we found (relevant to issue X1). Low reliability (issue N1); use at most as a bound
in a sensitivity analysis.

## Numbers (Table 1, mean ± SD, 12 fresh male cadavers, 35–40 y, H&E)

| Level | Perineurium (µm) | "Epineurium" (µm) | Fascicles "per nerve trunk" |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mid arm (coracobrachialis insertion) | 2.60 ± 2.42 (text: ± 0.42) | 3.3 ± 2.1 | 9.50 ± 1.29 |
| Cubital fossa (upper border of pronator teres; called "forearm") | 6.08 ± 1.35 | 8.36 ± 3.53 | 8.75 ± 1.71 |
| Wrist (proximal flexor retinaculum) | 2.25 ± 0.15 | 4.52 ± 2.07 | 8.38 ± 2.17 |

With Brill & Tyler fascicle diameters (0.47–0.6 mm), the perineurium is ≈ 0.4–1.3% of the
diameter (our computation, different specimens): closer to Horn's ≈ 1% than to the 3% rule.

## Caveats (issue N1)

- Epineurium of 3–8 µm is incompatible with an epineurium that fills > 50% of the section; the
  measured layer is probably not the whole epineurium.
- Fascicle counts were done "in 10 high-power fields", about half the whole-section counts of
  other studies.
- Arm perineurium SD differs between table (2.42) and text (0.42).
