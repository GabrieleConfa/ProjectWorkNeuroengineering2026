# Horn et al. 2023 — Electrical properties of mammalian peripheral nerve laminae

**Citation.** Horn MR, Vetter C, Bashirullah R, Carr M, Yoshida K. *Characterization of the
Electrical Properties of Mammalian Peripheral Nerve Laminae.* Artif. Organs 47(4):705–720, 2023.
doi:10.1111/aor.14500. File: `Papers/nihms-1921271.pdf` (NIH author manuscript).

**Role in the project.** Main source for **phase 2 (electrical properties)**: the first direct
measurement on a mammalian nerve of the resistivity and permittivity of perineurium, epineurium
and (longitudinal) endoneurium. Until this paper nearly every model used the 1984 frog-nerve
values of Weerasuriya et al.

## What they did

- 5 canine **vagus** nerves, ex vivo, Krebs solution, **room temperature 22 ± 1 °C**.
- Four-point impedance spectroscopy, 10 mHz – 50 kHz:
  - radial: current from a needle inside the major fascicle to a cylindrical Ag/AgCl wall;
    the perineurium is then punctured repeatedly to separate its contribution from the
    epineurium's (two semicircles in the Cole–Cole plot, fitted recursively);
  - longitudinal: two intrafascicular wires moved 0–12 mm apart → resistance per unit length.
- Histology of the same segments to get geometry; cylindrical formulas → bulk ρ and ε_r.
- COMSOL model of the chamber with the mean values to test whether linear bulk parameters reproduce
  the measured spectra.

## Take-aways for our model

- The perineurium **sheet resistance agrees with the frog values** → the classic resistive
  parameter set is acceptable for a mammalian nerve. Electrically what matters is ρ·t: Horn's
  thin (≈ 14 µm), very resistive layer and Zelechowski's thicker (3% of diameter), less
  resistive layer can give a similar barrier. Compare both through ρ·t in `docs/parameters.md`.
- Epineurium measured here (≈ 0.018 S/m) is ≈ 4.5× less conductive than the 0.0826 S/m used by
  Zelechowski 2020 — a candidate for the sensitivity analysis.
- Endoneurium longitudinal ≈ 1 S/m vs 0.571 S/m in Zelechowski; but Horn computed it with the
  whole nerve diameter (2.2 mm, including epineurium), so it is not a clean endoneurium value.
- **Capacitive effects**: corner frequencies (perineurium ≈ 2.6 kHz) fall within the spectrum of
  short pulses (a 50 µs pulse has content up to ≈ 10–20 kHz). A purely resistive (quasi-static)
  model ignores this — state it as a limitation, or justify it.

## Caveats

- Canine **vagus** (autonomic, small) ≠ human median; ex vivo; room temperature (conductivity
  rises with temperature; Q10 corrections are themselves uncertain, p. 3).
- No transverse endoneurium value is given (only longitudinal).
- Histology was distorted by the punctures; epineurial geometry is uncertain (large SD on ε_r).
- **Sheet capacitance inconsistent by a factor 100** (our check, 2026-10-19): with the paper's
  own formula C_s = ε0·ε_r/d, the mean values give 8.85e-12 × 2018 / 13.8e-6 m ≈ 1.3e-3 F/m²
  = **0.13 µF/cm²**, not 13.5. The same ×100 shows for every segment (e.g. B91029R: 0.14 vs 14.3).
  With 0.13 µF/cm², R_s·C_s ≈ 65 µs → f_c ≈ 2.4 kHz (reported 2.6 kHz), while 13.5 µF/cm² would
  give ≈ 23 Hz. Not corrected by us: kept as reported and listed as an open question for the
  tutor (issue H1 in `Papers/main.tex`, Appendix A). If the factor 100 is confirmed, C_s would
  match Weerasuriya's *smaller* capacitance (0.08 µF/cm²), which would affect the paper's claim
  that Weerasuriya misattributed it (issue H2).
- Perineurium time constant ≈ 60 µs ≈ a 50 µs pulse width: the quasi-static resistive model
  probably overestimates the perineurial barrier during the pulse.
- The reported SD of ρ_L (0.051 Ω·m) is much narrower than the spread visible in Fig. 9
  (points ≈ 0.5–1.6 Ω·m); treat ± 0.05 with caution. Fig. 8 slope (≈ 0.26 kΩ/mm) × area of a
  2.2 mm circle does reproduce ρ_L ≈ 0.98 Ω·m.
- Fig. 10: the "literature" models are ≈ 5× too low because ρ = 476 Ω·m was applied to a 13.8 µm
  layer (R_s ≈ 66 Ω·cm² instead of ≈ 500) — a demonstration that ρ·t, not ρ, must be carried over.
- Fig. 2 labels the second Cole element "Endoneurium" although it holds the epineurium elements.
- Other minor inconsistencies: COMSOL RMS error 159 Ω / 20.7° (abstract, Table 4) vs
  191.7 Ω / 15.2° (Results); fascicle diameter SD ± 0.23 (abstract) vs ± 0.5 mm (Table 1);
  discussion says "three million" for the epineurium ε_r, table mean is 9.4·10⁶.
- If the longitudinal resistance is referred to the fascicle area (1.3 mm) instead of the whole
  nerve (2.2 mm), ρ_L ≈ 0.34 Ω·m (σ ≈ 3 S/m, above saline, so implausible): the true endoneurium
  value lies in between and is sensitive to the assumed area.

## Newer literature (checked 2026-10-21)

No erratum and no comparable new measurement of mammalian/human laminae found. Summarised in
`Papers/main.tex` (Horn chapter, "What has been published since 2023"): `pelot_2019.md`
(temperature/frequency-corrected perineurium values; Zelechowski's 1136 Ω·m ≈ frog value at
37 °C, which partly explains X5), `davis_2023.md` (perineurium thickness vs fascicle diameter,
ASCENT parameter set), `lazorchak_2022.md` (capacitive laminae matter mainly for short pulses;
issue Q2). Also cited: Krug 2025, Acta Biomater. 199:290–300 (rat epi-perineurium barrier
measured in a miniaturised Ussing chamber; values not checked, not open access).
